
**How Much is Unknown?** Filmmaking is a balance of rigid planning and
controlled improvisation.

* Planned: The story, dialogue, locations, and general visual style are
thoroughly understood and planned via the script, storyboards, and shot lists
before filming begins. * Unknown/Fluid: The exact execution often changes on
set. A planned close-up might not work with the lighting, or an actor's
improvisation might inspire a new angle. The "unknown" is usually the smaller or
even micro decision making required to adapt the plan to reality, not the
fundamental story beats.

In summary, the screenplay provides the blueprint, the director and DP design
the coverage (the specific shots), and pickups are a safety net for minor fixes,
not a substitute for proper planning.

A spec. or narrative script does not have much for production. A shooting script
is the technical blueprint for production.

The app should acknowledge that Production Mode is about managing complexity and
change, not just enforcing a static list established during pre-production.



1. Improvisational Camera Moves Sometimes the unplanned nature comes from the
execution rather than the shot list.

On-Set Discovery: A director might arrive at the location, see a unique
reflection or angle not visible in pre-production, and immediately instruct the
camera operator to capture it. This is often called "finding the shot" on the
day. Reaction to Improv: If actors improvise dialogue or business (action), the
camera operator might instinctively pan or zoom to follow the new action,
creating a unique shot that deviates from the static plan.

2. "Wild" Shots and Coverage While "wild" usually refers to audio (recorded
without camera), the concept applies to video too. Directors often shoot extra
coverage or safety shots that were not in the shooting script or storyboard.

The "Safety" Take: A director might finish the planned shots and then say,
"Let's get a safety," meaning an extra angle or close-up just in case the edit
needs it. The "Freebie" or "Bonus" Shot: If time permits, the director might
quickly grab an artistic angle or insert that wasn't planned but looks
interesting on location.



Distinguishing Planned vs Captured Shots and Status Management** In professional
filmmaking, a significant portion of the final film often comes from unplanned
shots captured spontaneously during production. It means that the current Previs
Timeline, though tied to the storyboard and shot list, would have to allow for
some flexibility.

1. Distinguish "Planned" vs. "Captured" Shots In traditional production, a
Script Supervisor "lines" the script to track what was actually filmed versus
what was planned. Your UI should replicate this:

* Status Indicators: Mark shots in your script editor box outlines with status tags: Planned,
Generated, Modified, or Skipped. 
* On-the-Fly Addition: Allow users to instantly
add new shot boxes between existing numbered shots (e.g., adding 24B.5 for a
spontaneous close-up) without breaking the numbering sequence. This mimics a
director deciding to grab an extra angle on set.

These are distinct from "pickups" (which happen after production) and are
instead part of the on-set process.
