**Refined Feature Description (Aligned with Real Filmmaking Workflow):**

In the left margin of the script editor, display a vertical column divided into **Scene blocks**. Within each Scene block are nested **Shot blocks** (clearly labeled as Master Shot, Coverage, B-Roll, or Pickup). 

Each Shot block can be expanded to reveal its associated **Storyboard Frames** (one or more per shot). 

- Dragging the top or bottom edge of a Shot block or individual Storyboard Frame adjusts its approximate duration. These changes automatically update the overall estimated runtime in a connected **Storyboard Video Player** (scrub-able preview of the animatic/rough sequence).
- Clicking on a Shot block or specific Storyboard Frame highlights the corresponding section of the screenplay text on the right, scrolling the script to the relevant action lines or dialogue.
- Conversely, selecting text in the script automatically highlights the matching Shot and Frame in the left column.

This vertical column links bidirectionally to a **horizontal timeline view** at the bottom of the screen (toggleable). The timeline contains multiple tracks:

- **Script Track** – Visual representation of the screenplay text flowing across time, with Scene and Shot dividers.
- **Dialogue Track** – Precise timing blocks for spoken lines, allowing word-level adjustments.
- **Storyboard Frame Track** – Visual thumbnails of the storyboard panels sequenced horizontally, with their durations matching the left-margin blocks.
- **Sound Design / SFX Track** – Cues for sound effects, environmental ambiences (e.g., rain, data hums, breathing pulse tones).
- **Music / Atmos Track** – Layers for score or tonal beds that support the atmospheric tension.

**Key Workflow Adjustments for Realism:**

- Timing adjustments in this tool remain *pre-visualization* only — they create a rough animatic timing guide, not final locked edit timing (which happens later in NLE software like Premiere or DaVinci).
- Master Shots appear as longer foundational blocks, with Coverage and B-Roll as shorter, layered elements that can overlap or offer alternate options.
- Pickups are visually flagged (different color or tag) and can be inserted without disrupting the main sequence.
- The system maintains balance: rigid structural hierarchy on one side (Scenes → Shots → Frames) while allowing fluid creative flow on the other (easy reordering, duration tweaks, and visual preview).

This creates an elegant, unified workspace where the written word, visual planning, and temporal flow support each other naturally — structured enough for technical precision, yet flexible enough for those subtle, living adjustments that emerge during development.
